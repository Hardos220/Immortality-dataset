# Methodology

How the database was assembled. This section describes the actual procedure, not
an ideal one: it is a curated aggregation, not a systematic review under PRISMA.

> 🇷🇺 Русская версия — [`methodology.md`](methodology.md)

## What this is, as a genre

**Expert curated data aggregation.** Not a systematic review: there is no
registered protocol, search strings were recorded as the work went on, screening
was done by one person, and no formal risk-of-bias assessment was carried out.

If you plan to use this database in a systematic review or a meta-analysis, treat
it as a **starting point for searching**, not as the result of systematic
selection.

## Boundaries of the subject

Indicators were included if they bore on any of these topics:

- attitudes to radical life extension and immortality;
- desired, expected and actual lifespan;
- attitudes to life-extension technologies (geroprotectors, cloning, organ
  transplantation);
- healthy living as life extension actually practised;
- demographic statistics of longevity and of the limits to lifespan;
- cryonics, digital immortality, the markets of the longevity industry;
- historical forecasts of the limit to lifespan.

There were no geographic restrictions. Languages: Russian and English. Time span:
attitude measurements from 1973; demographic series begin in 1751 (Sweden, life
tables); historical statements about life extension from 1780. The upper bound is
July 2026.

## How sources were searched for

The search proceeded iteratively, across five thematic blocks:

| Block | What was searched for |
|---|---|
| A | International surveys: Pew, Ipsos, YouGov, Gallup, Eurobarometer, ESS, ISSP, Wellcome |
| B | Russian: ВЦИОМ, Левада-Центр, Росстат, ФОМ, Russian Field |
| C | Demography: UN WPP, WHO, Eurostat, national statistical offices, peer-reviewed work |
| D | Healthy living and health: WHO, The Lancet, McKinsey, Global Wellness Institute |
| E | Cryonics, digital immortality, industry reviews |

**Snowballing** was used in addition: the bibliographies of reviews found along
the way (Aparicio 2024 in particular) pointed to earlier work, which was then
searched for separately.

A full log of search queries with verdicts, and a log of URL retrievals, were kept
during the work and remain in the project’s working files.

## Extraction rules

For every indicator the following were recorded: country, year the data were
**collected** (not published), sample size, subgroup, the exact or abbreviated
wording of the question, the value and the unit.

Priority went to the primary source. Where it was unavailable (paywall, server
refusal), the indicator was taken from a secondary source and `source_access` was
set to `secondary` — such rows are marked explicitly and require checking before
publication.

Figures were transferred verbatim. Recalculations were performed only where the
`note` field or the chart caption says so.

## Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The database does **not reject** sources on quality — it **labels** them. The
reason lies in the genre: this is a map of the field, and its empty or weak places
matter no less than its strong ones. Discard everything imperfect and the main
finding disappears — that for most countries and most rungs of the “ladder” there
simply are no decent data.

**Included** if all three conditions hold:

1. There is a **verifiable numeric value** — a percentage, an age, a count, a
   score. Qualitative work is included only if a number can be extracted from it
   (the number of interviews conducted, for example).
2. The **primary source** is known: organisation or authors, year, and either a
   URL, a DOI or an unambiguous bibliographic reference.
3. The **year of data collection** is known, or the period the value refers to.
   A publication year without a collection year is admissible only for population
   statistics.

**Excluded:**

- values with no traceable primary source — above all figures that circulate
  through the press without a reference (this is how estimates of the number of
  users of digital-immortality services were thrown out);
- values **generated by models and presented as measurement**. One case is
  recorded separately: an industry summary of the cryonics market where the
  authors themselves stated that the figures were obtained by “speculative
  consensus based on LLM analysis”;
- commercial market-size estimates with undisclosed methodology (report-mill);
- duplicates: one and the same measurement retold by several outlets is entered
  once, with a reference to the primary source.

Non-probability samples, interested commissioners and preprints are **not
excluded**; they receive tier T3 or T2 and an explicit remark in the `notes`
field. A user of the dataset can filter them out in one line:
`df[df.source_tier == "T1"]`.

## What to do with figures that contradict one another

The database does not pick a single “right” number. The order of operations is
as follows.

1. **First it is checked whether this really is the same indicator.** In most of
   the diverging pairs it turned out that different things were being measured.
   An example: the share who want to live forever and the share opposed to
   immortality, from one and the same study — this is not 16 % against 56 % but
   two different questions; in the first version of the database they were
   wrongly merged into one indicator.
2. **If the indicator really is one and the figures diverge, both are kept**, each
   with its own `source_id`, and a cross-reference to the discrepancy is put in
   `note`. Averaging is not applied: the mean of two incomparable methodologies
   means nothing.
3. **If the discrepancy is explained by the method, it is recorded as a finding
   in its own right, not as noise.** An example: adherence to the physical
   activity guideline in the United States is 23 % by self-report and 0.3 % by
   accelerometer on one and the same sample. Such pairs are the most valuable
   part of the database.
4. **If one of the values turns out to be an artefact of retelling, it is removed,
   and the indicator number is not reused.** This is how the figure “95 % against
   digital resurrection without consent” was removed, having failed to hold up
   against the primary publication.

## Heterogeneity of the data: how it is handled

The data are knowingly heterogeneous across countries, years, samples and
wordings. A formal meta-analysis taken head-on is **not performed and not
planned** — pooling measurements of different constructs into one estimate would
be an error.

Instead:

- comparability is bounded by an explicit rule: only rows sharing the same
  `indicator` may be compared (see `data/codebook.md`);
- every indicator is coded by question type, technology type, answer format and
  the presence of a proviso in the wording — which allows the comparable to be
  grouped and makes visible where a comparison is illegitimate;
- known non-comparable pairs are listed by name in the codebook;
- the coverage matrix shows which “country × topic” cells are empty, so that
  absence of data is not mistaken for absence of the phenomenon.

A meta-regression of the effect of wording on the answer is a task for future
work; the `framing` and `question_type` fields were added to the dataset for
exactly that.

## Source tiers

Tiers were assigned by expert judgement, by a single coder, without computing
agreement. This is a **subjective assessment**, not the result of applying a
validated instrument.

- **T1** — a representative national sample or official statistics; direct
  measurement on the subject;
- **T2** — an adjacent subject, a commercial survey, or a limited or incompletely
  described methodology;
- **T3** — a non-probability sample, industry analysis, reference publications.

If you need a defensible quality assessment, apply a formal instrument for
cross-sectional studies (AXIS, for example) with two independent coders.

## Verification

Indicators originally taken from second-hand accounts were checked as a separate
stage. Four critical sources were traced back to the primary document, which
produced three substantial corrections:

- the indicator “95 % against grief-bots without consent” **was not confirmed**
  and was removed as an error of retelling;
- for the Russian survey on cloning, the refusal shares and the breakdowns by
  generation and settlement type were obtained, absent from the press coverage;
- the Rosstat healthy-lifestyle methodology was confirmed, and a breakdown by sex
  was added.

Also, while working with converted text it was found that in one source two
different indicators (the share wanting to live forever and the share opposed to
immortality) were presented as one — this has been corrected.

## Data processing

The published CSV files are assembled by `scripts/build_dataset.py`, which:

1. maps country names to ISO 3166-1 alpha-3 and sets the type of coverage;
2. parses values of the form `20-24`, `>50`, `150-190` into
   `value_min` / `value_max` / `value_type`;
3. parses periods of the form `2021-2023`, `до 1800` into `year_start` /
   `year_end`;
4. attaches to every indicator the source name, link and tier;
5. recomputes the coverage matrix.

The script is deterministic: the same input yields the same output.

## Calculations used in the showcase

The few derived quantities, and their formulae:

- **Confidence intervals**: ±1.96·√(p(1−p)/n), the normal approximation. Computed
  only where the sample size is published.
- **The record life-expectancy line**: a linear trend between two documented
  points (Sweden 1840 — 46 years; Hong Kong 2021 — 88 years), which matches the
  published finding of linear growth. The moments of crossing the declared limits
  are computed from that trend.
- **The ratio of declaration to action**: the share declaring the desire,
  multiplied by the world population, divided by the number of cryopreservation
  contracts signed. The quantity is ordinal; precision here is illusory.
- **An illustrative conjunction calculation**: 0.6⁵ ≈ 7.8 % — it shows what part
  of the gap between self-reported healthy living and its measurement is created
  by the composite nature of the index.

## What was not done

- No meta-analysis was performed and no pooled effects were computed.
- No sensitivity analysis or publication-bias assessment was carried out.
- Microdata were not requested from the authors of the studies.
- Primary questionnaires were not translated or recoded.

All of the above belongs to the next stage of the work.
